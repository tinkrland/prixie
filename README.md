# video

video is prixie's face. some meetings do not want a camera-off black square. video gives prixie an on-screen presence: a talking avatar generated by masky.ai, synced to whatever she is saying (the voice side lives on the voice branch).

the proskena-derived pieces live in studioify/ (the plate builder that speaks proskena's scene-state schema, the pinhole camera, the sets and light recipes, the headless renderer), and studioify/roadmap.md holds the direction: interactive props from sam 3d segmented objects, prop state in her persona memory, stage cues through the speak-turn pipeline.

why decoupled: the join engine (main) and the voice (voice) do not care what she looks like. video only matters where the platform renders a face, and it plugs in at the camera stream level once the other layers are solid.

status: wired. server/masky.ts talks to the real api (https://masky.ai/masky-api.md) and the full flow is smoke-tested live: portrait image -> prixie avatar -> conversation -> speak turn -> lip-synced 6s h264 clip, ~20s end to end, ~$0.16 in credits. camera publishing is wired too (server/lk_publisher.ts, the owner oct 21 2026): the render demuxes with ffmpeg -re (native-rate pacing, the pipe IS the clock) and publishes into the livekit room as her camera and audio tracks; rtc-node loads lazily, geometry from ffprobe, publishFaceOff stays with yap.

## flow: one voice, two rooms

one call uses livekit for voice and masky for the face, and they must not drift.
so there is only one voice: the speak turn renders higgs tts (the avatar's cloned
voice) and the lip-sync together and hands back both files.

```
retelldb (persona memory)          what she knows, how she says it
        |                          voice profile -> voiceStyle cues
        v
masky speak turn (text in, ~20-60s, cached per script, draft by default)
        |
        +--> audioUrl --> livekit audio track   (what the room hears)
        +--> videoUrl --> livekit camera track  (what the room sees)
        |
        +--> idle clips between turns (silent renders, blinks, head motion)
```

the room hears exactly the render the mouth is moving to, by construction, not
by syncing two providers. masky owns the voice whenever she is on camera; voice's
own tts covers face-off turns only. server/handoff.ts is that seam, and voice
implements one interface (RoomPublisher.publish) to take both tracks.

seams:

- server/masky.ts: ensurePersonaAvatar (one face per persona), startConversation,
  speak, waitForClip, enableIdleLoop, makePublic, bindAgentBrain
- server/handoff.ts: PersonaVoice (face + voice + memory as one object), say(),
  recall(), runCall(); the delivery mapping lives in persona/voice_adapter.ts
- the persona context is synced from the context branch (retelldb/ +
  persona/ via `sh tools/sync-context.sh`): the context branch owns the
  context, this branch carries the byte-identical copy, and the voice
  adapter (persona/voice_adapter.ts, the one profile -> delivery cues
  mapping) is the same file the voice branch uses, so her voice and memories
  are the same everywhere.

## cost notes

billed per second of finished render, once, on success. live rates from
GET /api/videos/pricing: 720p is 0.025/s full and 0.015/s draft, 1080p is
0.05/s full and 0.03/s draft, portraits 0.005, 1 credit = $1. draft is the
default on every turn here (a meeting face is presence, not a render). scripted
speak turns are cached: the same line in the same voice renders once, replays
are free.

## twitch, kick, and the rest (integrations/)

integrations/ holds the places her stream goes. obs.ts is the bridge
(obs-websocket 5.x, ours, not masky's) and it is platform-agnostic on
purpose: a platform is just an rtmp destination, one file each. twitch.ts
is the stable public ingest; kick.ts takes kick's ingest url + key from its
creator dashboard (kick has no mature public api for chat or channel info,
and nothing pretends otherwise). adding a platform is adding a file: no
bridge changes, no platform lock-in.

masky already ships twitch and OBS integration natively (avatars can be
twitch-owned, ids show up as twitch:123, and the live url drops straight into
OBS). for streamer presence that path needs none of this: it is the shortcut.
the livekit track handoff above is for meeting joins, where she has to be a
participant with a camera and a mic, not a browser source.
